# Arm transfer pilot: interface and provenance

The plant is a 0.30 m + 0.20 m planar two-revolute-joint kinematic arm, not a torque or motor dynamics model. A nominal affine camera has identity matrix and zero offset, so nominal camera units equal base-frame metres. The evaluator may rotate the camera, change actuator gains, and add paired camera noise. Joint increments are radians, independently bounded to ±0.1 rad, and applied for 0.1 simulated seconds; nominal motion is 0.8 times the sent increment. There is no transport delay, dropped frame, backlash or joint-state sensor.

Each method receives camera-space tip position and target, its prior sent commands and timestamps. It receives no joint angles, executed motion, camera transform, disturbance label or change timestamp. The task-facing desired behavior is `0.7 * (target - observation)`, bounded to a 0.025 camera-unit Euclidean displacement per 0.1 s. The nominal inverse maps this displacement to bounded joint increments using a damped kinematic Jacobian. Its nominal lengths, response and identity camera are prior model assumptions, common to all methods; inverse kinematics selects the negative-elbow branch. The analytical nominal model is given without charged calibration, equally for all methods; it is an idealized prior, not measured calibration. No probes are included in this pilot. Positive-elbow or out-of-workspace observations can violate the prior; clipping the inverse-kinematics cosine does not resolve ambiguity.

The evaluator applies an intermediate target for 20 steps, then a final target for 100 steps. In disturbance cases the disturbance starts at the target switch; no-change cases have the same target schedule. Thus the pilot measures recovery during a scheduled task change and cannot isolate a stationary-target fault response. All methods receive the same per-case noise and fixed 120-action budget. Reachability is checked using evaluator truth and the post-change camera, for the configured full joint limits. The camera jump is visible and can be mistaken for action response; no controller receives an explicit change flag.

## Transfer from FLAIR

The reference is the authors' [FLAIR v1.0.0 release](https://zenodo.org/records/18336476), revision `9e1fa31a8f39b912c5edb61bd65ba15289241276`, especially `fast_adaptation/example/flair_example.py` and `fast_adaptation/src/flair/functionality_controller/`. This is an independent, simplified **FLAIR-style transfer**, not the authors' implementation.

| Component | Released example | Arm pilot |
|---|---|---|
| Global command–behavior model | Parametric differential-drive-inspired mean function. | Adapt a 2×2 map from nominal predicted camera displacement to observed displacement by recursive least squares. The nominal arm Jacobian depends on inferred configuration. |
| Local correction | GP residual on the command–behavior map. | Bounded FIFO of 24 samples and an exact squared-exponential GP residual over normalized camera position and predicted displacement. The global-only ablation omits this residual. |
| Selection and timing | Command/sensor buffers, delay matching, filtering and dataset management. | Pair each issued command with the next observation at a fixed synchronous simulation step; discard only near-zero commands/predictions. No delay matcher, outlier filter or grid-balanced selection. |
| Detection/reset | Error-buffer based automatic reset is available. | No change detector or reset; models persist across the target switch and fault. This omission may contaminate adaptation and must not be presented as an implementation of FLAIR reset. |
| Inversion | Chooses a correction from a modeled command repertoire. | Regularized inverse of the learned global map, with one residual correction, then the same nominal joint inverse. It is not a full nonlinear GP inversion. |

The online-Jacobian baseline fits command-to-camera displacement directly by RLS and applies a damped inverse. Its forgetting factor and damping are tuned on development cases. All methods use the same desired displacement cap, actuator bounds, observations and action horizon. The baseline does not use the GP or the transfer model's configuration-dependent nominal Jacobian after initialization; this pilot does not establish that it is the strongest possible Jacobian controller.

Error is distance to target in camera units. Integrated post-change error is the sum times 0.1 s divided by a fixed 0.5 camera-unit workspace scale. Recovery requires five consecutive observations within 0.015 units, measured to the fifth observation; failures retain null recovery time. Peak excursion is maximum observed displacement from the first post-change observation, separately from peak target error. Action counts include all 20 pre-change and 100 post-change movements. Controller computation is measured host wall time; task/recovery times are simulated. Each episode stores its case, seed, metrics and trajectory. Evaluation parameters and distinct held-out poses, targets, gains and camera angles are recorded in `results/arm_transfer/frozen_evaluation.json` before evaluation.

## Source checks and limits

The paper table was reproduced by running the authors' analysis on their archived 20-run data, not by running 20 new simulations. Three unpaired new author-example runs show the same qualitative ordering using the author metric, but cannot reproduce the population result. The author's `-wz*pi/180` conversion is the convention needed to reproduce their table; the released simulator's body-z angular rate behaves like rad/s in a short quaternion-derived yaw-rate check. Historical preprocessing and coordinate convention are not established, so the numerical author-metric comparisons are not treated as physically validated. Their synthetic timestamps advance 0.02 s per five 0.04 s physics steps; the mismatch can change data selection. The arm pilot uses explicit 0.1 s synchronous command/observation steps and does not depend on the contested angular metric. Physical timing and sensor alignment remain untested.
