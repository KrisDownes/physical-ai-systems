# SO-101 camera-shift diagnosis

The original `49ac12f` configurations, results, charts and recordings were copied to `../results/so101_archive/baseline_49ac12f/` before new runs; their SHA-256 manifest is retained. Original committed artifacts are unchanged. New evaluation commands write to unique timestamp/revision/UUID directories under `../results/so101_runs/`, with frozen configuration, seeds, source hashes and revision in `metadata.json`. An interrupted evaluation was resumed only after checking the frozen configuration and control-source hashes; its per-episode records were not overwritten. Two exploratory records from a separate interrupted run were excluded from the fresh results. The first development marker detector incorrectly included pixels from the blue destination; the detector was corrected and tested before the reported runs. The moving-jaw body-name exclusion in the first version of the physics audit was also corrected before the fresh evaluation. Both earlier attempts are retained locally and are not part of the reported comparison.

## Paired trace and interventions

The paired case uses the same initial object position `[0.217, 0.021]` m and arm configuration with either zero camera translation or `[+0.012, -0.008]` m. A non-colliding cyan gripper-mounted simulated marker is added for visualization and the self-calibration comparator; it does not alter the arm dynamics. The [paired overlay](../results/so101_runs/20261114T015540.657337Z_49ac12f199cf_camera_diagnosis_0e463eab/paired_before_close.png) and [online camera-shift recording](../results/so101_runs/20261114T015540.657337Z_49ac12f199cf_camera_diagnosis_0e463eab/shift_online_overlay.gif) show detected object (green), detected gripper marker (cyan), commanded target (red) and tip (blue). Red and blue are **evaluator projections using the actual simulation camera**, not observations given to the controller; cyan is the marker, not the fingertip itself.

The earliest divergence is at the image-to-world transformation, before any adaptation action: the object estimate is biased by `[-13.58, +7.48]` mm in the shifted case, compared with `[-1.05, +0.64]` mm without the shift. The detection and camera projection itself are not sufficient to explain the gap: substituting an evaluator-projected exact object pixel while retaining stale calibration still produced a `[-12, +8]` mm error and failed. The grasp target is constructed once from the first image plus a fixed grasp offset and is never updated during the episode.

With the shifted camera, the online controller's last recorded reach action put its tip approximately 1.35 mm from its **biased commanded waypoint**. Immediately before closure, the commanded tip was `[0.1918, 0.0161, 0.0140]` m, the observed simulated tip was `[0.1922, 0.0166, 0.0152]` m, and the object center was `[0.2170, 0.0210, 0.0199]` m. The commanded grasp height was 14 mm; the lateral tip-to-object offset changed from approximately `[-13.0, -11.8]` mm without the camera shift to `[-24.8, -4.4]` mm with it. The jaw was commanded from 1.2 rad open to -0.1 rad: in the failure it reached about -0.100 rad and had no object contact; in the nominal grasp it stopped around +0.116 rad against the object and both jaws made contact. Thus the traced arm approximately followed the wrong target and closed beside the object. These truth values are evaluator diagnostics, not controller inputs.

Before grasp, online RLS accepted 10 command/joint-response updates, and global/GP accepted 10/16 FK-predicted/observed displacement updates. The input matrices had numerical ranks 4/5, 3/3 and 3/3 respectively for this trajectory; a rank count alone does not prove adequate excitation or generalization. Crucially, none receives a camera-to-object calibration measurement after initial localization. Last recorded reach-site errors were 1.35 mm online, 1.67 mm global and 1.86 mm GP; all three failed contact. Command-to-motion adaptation therefore cannot correct this fixed target bias using its existing inputs, even though it receives movement data.

An explicitly oracle-labeled **target** correction produced contact, lift and placement for both nominal and online methods under the same shifted camera. The unshifted nominal and a marker-based self-calibration run also succeeded with simulated contact. These interventions isolate target-coordinate bias as the earliest supported cause for this case; they do not prove the grasp geometry is robust across other objects or that a perfect motion model was identified. Split controller/evaluator logs, per-step model updates, recorded detections, contact snapshots and all interventions are in the diagnosis run directory.

## Fresh paired evaluation

The [fresh protocol](frozen_camera_followup.json) froze four new object/start cases and two new camera translations before execution. Each shifted case used identical initial conditions for each method and reference mode. The non-colliding cyan marker is assumed rigidly attached to the gripper on a 0.11 m simulated mast and its pose is known from the nominal encoder/FK model. The self-calibration comparator estimates camera translation from its detected pixel and known 3D marker position; this is a conventional, idealized camera/robot registration baseline, not an active-learning contribution. All methods receive the same images and joint readings.

| Condition | Fixed nominal | Online Jacobian | Table-marker recalibration | Gripper-marker self-calibration |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| No camera change, markers visible | 2/2 | 2/2 | 2/2 | 2/2 |
| Camera shift, table markers visible | 3/4 | 2/4 | 4/4 | 4/4 |
| Camera shift, markers temporarily hidden | 3/4 | 2/4 | 4/4 | 4/4 |
| Camera shift, markers unavailable | 3/4 | 2/4 | 0/4 | 4/4 |

All 56 episodes had the object and gripper marker detectable in the initial image. The table-marker method's four unavailable-reference failures are explicit **calibration failures**, not grasp failures; it waits for six charged 0.1 s actions, then stops. Temporary hiding suppresses both reference marker images for the first 0.5 s after reset and then restores them; unavailable suppresses them throughout. This is an image-level visibility mask, not a physically modeled occluder. The two table markers have known world positions. The gripper marker and object are not masked and are initially visible in every condition; ordinary occlusion later in the movement is not ruled out. A single initial frame plus known FK, camera orientation and marker height makes the translation identifiable in this ideal model. If both world references and a known gripper feature are unavailable, the passive motion-only models in this experiment do not observe the absolute object-to-arm offset; that does not rule out other image-based visual servo approaches.

All methods share a maximum of 200 actions and 8 rad cumulative joint movement, with budget checking before each step (at most one bounded-action overshoot). Every wait and move is charged. The largest observed run used 91 actions and 2.673 rad. No joint-limit violations were recorded. The evaluator checked robot-internal contacts at **each of 87,700 physics steps**, including reset settling and all controlled steps, with intended gripper–object contacts excluded by body roots and jaw-to-jaw closure excluded; it recorded zero prohibited self-collision steps across these 56 episodes. This is a model/contact audit, not a hardware safety guarantee.

The comparator solved these translation cases without an exploratory movement: it exploited an already visible, accurately modeled gripper marker. On this evidence, a task-aware probe is not justified yet. A useful next test is to measure sensitivity to gripper-marker occlusion, marker-to-tool calibration error and camera rotation on **new paired cases**, while keeping table-marker recalibration and visual feedback as strong baselines. Only if passive observations cannot distinguish competing mappings should we specify a bounded probe and compare it with passive control and a fixed probe under equal budgets. Avoid introducing combined disturbances until the individual failure is understood.

Four deterministic shifted cases and two no-change cases per method are a small pilot, not a statistical reliability claim or evidence of physical transfer. No RL training was run.

## Reproduction

From `projects/active_robot_adaptation`, with `.venv-so101` and the pinned model set up as described in `README.md`:

```sh
MUJOCO_GL=egl PYTHONPATH=so101 OPENBLAS_NUM_THREADS=1 .venv-so101/bin/python so101/diagnose.py
MUJOCO_GL=egl PYTHONPATH=so101 OPENBLAS_NUM_THREADS=1 .venv-so101/bin/python so101/camera_followup.py
MUJOCO_GL=egl PYTHONPATH=so101 OPENBLAS_NUM_THREADS=1 .venv-so101/bin/python -m unittest discover -s so101 -p 'test_*.py' -v
```

Each command that writes evaluation output creates a new run directory. Resume an interrupted camera-follow-up run with `camera_followup.py --resume RUN_DIRECTORY`; it checks the saved configuration and control-source hashes and skips completed episodes. `compact_followup.py --run RUN_DIRECTORY` creates a readable summary of each episode plus lossless compressed full traces. The [fresh results](../results/so101_runs/20261114T015619.662436Z_49ac12f199cf_fresh_camera_followup_1aff5aad/summary.json) and [success chart](../results/so101_runs/20261114T015619.662436Z_49ac12f199cf_fresh_camera_followup_1aff5aad/success_rates.png) include every failure and visibility check.
